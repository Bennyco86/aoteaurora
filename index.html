The background

Built south-first.

Most aurora tools answer a space-weather question. AOTEAURORA starts with the question a person in New Zealand actually has: is it worth going outside from here, tonight?

An active Sun is only part of the answer. The aurora also has to reach far enough north, the sky needs to be dark, cloud has to cooperate, and moonlight can change what your eyes or camera will see.

How it works

One viewing answer.
Four layers of evidence.

AOTEAURORA turns specialist signals into a practical conditions rating. It does not pretend that a forecast is a guarantee.

01

Read the solar wind

Kp, Bz, field strength, speed and density help describe how much geomagnetic energy may be available.

02

Check whether it can reach

Your New Zealand location and the southern auroral oval turn a global event into a locally useful threshold.

03

Look through the atmosphere

Low, middle and high cloud, visibility and haze can decide whether an active aurora is actually observable.

04

Find the dark window

Night length, the Moon and changing conditions are combined to identify the strongest viewing window.

≈

A useful distinction

Conditions, not certainty.

Aurora forecasting is naturally uncertain. AOTEAURORA rates the evidence available for viewing; it does not claim to calculate a guaranteed probability that you will see an aurora.

More than a number

See the cause, not just the score.

The app is designed to help curious observers understand the chain from the Sun to the southern sky—not merely show a green or red badge.

The science underneath

Built on trusted public observations.

The app brings several specialist feeds together and interprets them for viewing in New Zealand.

01

NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center

Solar-wind measurements, geomagnetic information, the southern auroral oval and forecast products.

Visit NOAA SWPC ↗
02

GeoNet / Earth Sciences New Zealand

Arrival Heights geomagnetic observations, supplied by Earth Sciences New Zealand under CC BY 4.0, add regional context.

Visit GeoNet ↗
03

Open-Meteo

Local weather and air-quality forecast inputs, including cloud layers and atmospheric visibility.

Visit Open-Meteo ↗

Space-weather data: NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center. Geomagnetic data: GeoNet, supplied by Earth Sciences New Zealand under CC BY 4.0. Weather data: Open-Meteo. Solar and lunar geometry is calculated locally. These providers do not endorse or sponsor AOTEAURORA. Third-party observations and forecasts can be delayed, revised or temporarily unavailable.
